# Final Product Manager Summary

Planning data for the AAVA Integrations team is partially structured but lacks completeness in key areas required for reliable forecasting and portfolio decisions. While priorities are consistently captured at epic, feature, and story levels, epics have no Effort, Business Value, Time Criticality, or dates, and most features lack Business Value, Risk, and user cohort data. At the story level, only about 30% of items have Story Points, and start/due dates, release tags, UX expectations, and dependencies are mostly missing.

Because there is no explicit mapping of Azure DevOps states to “Completed,” and many stories within each iteration have no Story Points, it is currently impossible to compute a trustworthy **Current Completion Percentage — Not Historical Say-Do** for any iteration. This blocks accurate real-time measurement of sprint and PI progress and undermines the ability to compare planned vs. delivered scope.

Major risks include:

- Portfolio and PI planning decisions made without quantified value and effort at the epic/feature level.
- Iteration commitments based on partially estimated stories, especially in PI4 (Sprints 2, 4, 5, 6), which limits capacity and completion metrics.
- Release-level tracking compromised by missing Release Version and Planned Sprint data for many features and stories.

The three highest-priority corrective actions are:

1. **Establish and apply a completed-state mapping and estimation discipline.**  
   Define which story states are considered completed and ensure that all committed user stories have Story Points before sprint start (particularly in PI4 Sprints 2 and 4).

2. **Populate WSJF-style planning fields for epics and core features.**  
   For epics 51718, 51736, 70804, 70807 and key features (e.g., 51765, 53397, 58004), capture Effort, Business Value, Risk, Time Criticality, and Target Dates to enable value-based prioritization and trade-offs.

3. **Align release-roadmap work with sprints and user cohorts.**  
   Complete Release Version and Planned Sprint fields for release-bound features and stories, and map Primary User Cohort for features so that release planning reflects who benefits and when.

Implementing these steps will materially improve backlog readiness, sprint planning accuracy, and delivery predictability for the Integrations team.
